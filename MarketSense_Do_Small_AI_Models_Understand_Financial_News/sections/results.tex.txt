\section{Results}

\subsection{Headline Sentiment vs Same-Day Returns}
We regress same–day open$\rightarrow$close returns on daily headline sentiment computed by each model. Figures~\ref{fig:finbert_scatter_all} and \ref{fig:distil_scatter_all} show the pooled scatterplots (all tickers, all days). FinBERT exhibits a small but clear positive slope; DistilBERT shows no consistent relation. This visual pattern matches our statistics in Section~\ref{sec:summary_corr}.

\subsection{Per-Ticker Correlations}
\label{sec:summary_corr}
To summarize by name, we compute Pearson $r$ between sentiment and returns per ticker. Figure~\ref{fig:corr_bars} plots these values. FinBERT is positive for most symbols (notably AMZN, JPM, NVDA), while DistilBERT is mixed and often near zero. This supports the hypothesis that domain adaptation matters more than parameter count for this task.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figures/fig_corr_bars_by_ticker.png}
  \caption{Per-ticker Pearson correlation ($r$) between daily sentiment and same-day return, for FinBERT and DistilBERT.}
  \label{fig:corr_bars}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
Across pooled and per-ticker views, FinBERT’s sentiment correlates positively with same-day returns, while DistilBERT’s signal is weak or inconsistent. This pattern is robust to rolling aggregation and visible in multiple symbols.
